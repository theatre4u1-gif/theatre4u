Downsizing Checklist: Inventory Before You Sort

By the BinTrove team · November 27, 2026 · 7 min read

Downsizing a home is one of the harder things people do — not because of the boxes, but because every object carries a decision, and often a memory. The usual advice is to start sorting room by room. We'd suggest one step earlier: inventory first. Seeing everything before you decide anything makes the whole process calmer, fairer to the family, and far less likely to end in regret. Here's the checklist.

1. Inventory before you sort

Before a single thing moves to a "keep" or "donate" pile, walk each room and photograph what's there. It sounds like an extra step; it's the one that makes everything after it easier. With a visual list you can see the whole house at once, decide without the clock running, and avoid the trap of making a hundred emotional calls in one exhausting afternoon.

2. Measure the new space

Downsizing is, by definition, a smaller footprint. Get the room dimensions and storage of the new place and keep them handy. Now "will the hutch fit?" is a measurement, not a hope — and "we can keep this much, not that much" becomes a clear guide instead of a fight you have halfway through packing.

3. Sort into five, not three

With the inventory in hand, give each item a home:

  • Keep — it fits the new space and the new life.
  • Family — a keepsake going to a specific person.
  • Sell — worth something to someone else.
  • Donate — useful, but not to sell.
  • Toss — truly done.

The "family" category deserves its own pile because it's the one that causes the most heartache when it's rushed.

The easiest cuts first. Duplicates, anything that won't fit the measured space, and things untouched in years are low-stress decisions. Clear those early so the hard, sentimental sorting happens with less around it.

4. Share the inventory with family

Dividing keepsakes is where downsizing gets emotional. A photo inventory, shared with the family ahead of time, changes that. Relatives can look through the list and note what matters to them — calmly, on their own time — instead of standing in a half-packed house making claims under pressure. It turns a potential source of tension into a shared, fair process.

5. Label the keep boxes for the move

What survives the sort still has to move into the smaller home. Number and label every keep box and note what's inside and which room it's bound for. The move into a new, tighter space is exactly when you don't want to be hunting through unlabeled boxes — and the inventory you built at the start becomes the one you live with afterward.

Frequently asked questions

Where do I start when downsizing a home?

Start with an inventory, not a sorting spree. Photograph belongings room by room so you can see everything, measure what fits, and decide calmly instead of pile by pile.

How do I divide sentimental items among family when downsizing?

Make a photo inventory of the keepsakes and share it so each person can note what matters. Deciding from a calm list beforehand is fairer than choosing on the spot during the move.

What should I get rid of first when downsizing?

Duplicates, things that won't fit the new space, and items unused in years. Measure the new rooms so "will it fit?" is a fact, and save the sentimental sorting for when you have time and family input.
