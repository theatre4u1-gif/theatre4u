Storage Unit Inventory: Know What's Inside Without Driving Over

By the BinTrove team · November 27, 2026 · 6 min read

A storage unit has a way of becoming a black box you pay for every month. You know the holiday stuff is in there, and the old furniture, and… something else? When you actually need an item, you drive over, pull the door up, and spend half an hour shifting boxes to find it — or give up and buy a new one. A proper inventory turns that unit back into something you can use from your couch. Here's how.

Catalog it — all at once, or an aisle at a time

If you're loading a unit now, this is effortless: photograph each box and bin as it goes in. If the unit's already packed, don't try to redo it in one trip — do one section per visit. Pull a few boxes, photograph the contents, label them, restack, and you'll have the whole unit cataloged over a couple of visits without a brutal all-day session.

Number and label every box

The same system that works for moving works here:

  • A unique number on each box — written large, on more than one side.
  • A category so you can spot the right box from the doorway ("Kitchen," "Decor," "Kids — outgrown").

The number ties the box to a detailed list, so you don't have to cram every item onto the cardboard — and it lets you confirm nothing's gone missing between visits.

Draw a quick map

This is the storage-unit superpower nobody uses: note roughly where each numbered box lives. "1–8 front-left, 9–20 back wall, fragile on top." Now finding something isn't a dig — you check the list, see it's Box 14 on the back wall, and walk straight to it. A thirty-second map saves you a dozen half-hour searches.

Leave an aisle. When you pack the unit, keep a center path and put the things you'll want sooner near the front. A unit you can walk into is a unit you'll actually use.

Add QR labels for the long haul

Storage boxes sit for months or years — exactly where a handwritten label goes stale and a QR label shines. Stick a QR code on each box that links to its photo contents. On your next visit you scan a box and see everything inside without opening it, and when you add or pull something, you update the list once and the sticker stays accurate forever.

Keep it searchable — and note the valuables

The whole point is to search from home. With every box's contents in one place on your phone, "is the camping stove in storage or the garage?" is a quick lookup, and you know before you drive over. While you're cataloging, flag the higher-value items with photos and rough values: many homeowners and renters policies cover belongings in storage, and some facilities require insurance — either way, a photo inventory is what lets you file an accurate claim if the unit is broken into, flooded, or damaged.

Frequently asked questions

How do I keep track of what's in my storage unit?

Number and label every box, photograph its contents, and keep one searchable list of the whole unit (or put a QR code on each box). Then search an item from home, see which box it's in and roughly where it sits, and make one efficient trip.

Should I inventory my storage unit for insurance?

Yes — many policies cover belongings in storage, and some facilities require insurance. A photo inventory with rough values lets you file an accurate claim if the unit is damaged or broken into.

What's the best way to label storage unit boxes?

A unique number plus a category, written large and on more than one side. The number ties the box to a detailed list; a QR label makes the full contents one scan away.
