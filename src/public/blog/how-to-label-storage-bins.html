How to Label Storage Bins So You Can Actually Find Things Later

By the BinTrove team · November 25, 2026 · 6 min read

You did the hard part — you packed everything into matching bins and stacked them neatly in the garage. Then six months later you're standing in front of a wall of identical gray boxes, opening four of them to find one string of lights. The bins were never the problem. The labels were. Here's a simple system that keeps a bin's contents findable for months or years, without a label maker marathon.

Why most bin labels fail

A label fails for one of three reasons, and you've probably hit all of them:

  • It's too vague. "Garage stuff" or "Misc" describes every bin you own. A label has to narrow things down or it's just decoration.
  • It's on the wrong face. You labeled the lid, then stacked the bins so all you see are the sides. Now you're pulling the tower apart to read the tops.
  • It went stale. You added the beach towels and took out the winter gloves, but the marker label still says what was in there last spring. The label and the bin have quietly drifted apart.

Fix those three things and you've solved ninety percent of it. Here's how.

What a good bin label actually says

Keep it to two layers so a glance answers two different questions:

  • A category, big and on top. "Holiday — Christmas," "Camping," "Baby clothes 0–12mo." This answers which bin do I grab.
  • Three to five specific items underneath. "Tree stand, lights, stockings, ornaments." This answers is the thing I want actually in here, so you don't open it to check.

That's it. You don't need a full manifest handwritten on the side — just enough to point you to the right box and confirm the hit. Add the destination room only if it helps you unload after a move.

Group by category, not by room. Rooms change — especially during a move. A bin labeled "Guest Room" is useless the second it lands in the garage, but "Extra bedding" is true wherever it ends up.

The three ways to label — and when to use each

1. Handwritten

A thick marker on painter's tape or a blank label is perfectly good for bins you open all the time — the ones in constant rotation. It's free and instant. The catch: when the contents change, you have to remember to cross it out and rewrite it, and most people don't.

2. Printed

Printed labels look clean and are easy to read across a room, which is nice for a visible shelf. But they have the same weakness as handwriting: the moment the contents change, the printed label is wrong until you print a new one.

3. QR labels

For anything you won't open for months — holiday decor, seasonal clothes, keepsakes, the storage unit — a QR label is the one that doesn't go stale. The sticker never changes. What's behind it is a photo list of the contents that you update in an app. Add the beach towels, remove the gloves, and the next time you scan the bin, the current, complete list is right there on your phone. One sticker, always accurate.

How to set up a QR label system (the short version)

  1. Photograph what goes in the bin. A few quick snaps as you pack is enough — you'll recognize everything later from the pictures.
  2. Print the QR label and stick it on. Lid and at least two sides, so it's readable however the bin is stacked.
  3. Scan to see inside — anytime. Point your phone's camera at the bin and the full contents come up. No opening, no digging, no guessing.
  4. Update as things move. When you borrow the camping stove or add the new string lights, update the bin's list once. The label stays the same forever.

A 20-minute starter plan

Don't try to label the whole garage in one weekend — you'll burn out. Do this instead:

  1. Pick the three bins you most often go looking in (holiday, tools, whatever yours are).
  2. For each, snap a few photos of the contents and give it a clear category name.
  3. Print and stick a label — handwritten for the ones you open monthly, QR for the ones you open twice a year.
  4. Next time you're out there, do three more. In a few short sessions the whole room is done, and it stays done.

Keep one master list you can search

The real unlock isn't the sticker — it's being able to search. When every bin's contents live in one place on your phone, you stop hunting entirely: you type "extension cord," see it's in Bin 4 on the top shelf, and grab exactly that one. The labels get you to the right bin; the searchable list gets you to the right bin without even walking over.

Frequently asked questions

What should you write on a storage bin label?

Put a broad category at the top ("Holiday — Christmas") and three to five specific items below it ("tree stand, lights, stockings, ornaments"). The category tells you which bin to grab; the short list tells you whether the thing you want is inside without opening it.

Should you label bins by room or by category?

By category. Rooms change, especially during a move, and a "Guest Room" label is useless the moment that bin lands in the garage. A category like "Camping gear" or "Winter coats" stays true wherever the bin ends up.

Are QR labels better than handwritten ones?

For bins you open often, handwritten is fine. For long-term storage, a QR label is better because you never have to rewrite it — you update the contents in an app, and the same sticker always shows the current list, with photos, when you scan it.
