The Best Way to Store (and Actually Find) Holiday Decorations

By the BinTrove team · November 27, 2026 · 6 min read

The decorations always go up with excitement and come down with a sigh. Then they sit in the garage for eleven months, and next December you're hauling down six unlabeled bins to find the tree-topper, two of them turn out to be summer stuff, and the lights are a knotted mess. A little structure now — while it's all out and fresh in your mind — turns next year's setup into an evening instead of a weekend. Here's the system.

Sort by area, not by type

The instinct is to group by kind — all ornaments together, all lights together. Sort by where things go instead:

  • Tree — ornaments, tree skirt, topper, tree lights.
  • Mantel & living room — stockings, garland, figurines.
  • Outdoor — exterior lights, wreaths, yard decor, extension cords.
  • Table & kitchen — linens, serveware, centerpieces.

Now each bin maps to a part of the house. Decorating becomes "grab the Tree bin, do the tree; grab the Mantel bin, do the mantel." Teardown just reverses it, and nothing ends up homeless.

Protect the fragile things

The stuff that breaks is the stuff with the memories, so it's worth two extra minutes:

  • Ornaments: use a divided ornament bin, or wrap each one in packing paper. Keep breakables in their own clearly-labeled bin so nothing heavy ever lands on them.
  • Wreaths: a dedicated wreath bag or a round box keeps them from getting crushed flat.
  • Glass and ceramics: wrap, then pad the bin so nothing shifts in transit to and from storage.
Stack heavy-low, fragile-high. Sturdy bins (lights, linens, outdoor) go on the bottom; the ornament and glass bins ride on top. Put the "goes up first" bins where you can reach them without moving a wall of boxes.

Tame the lights once and for all

Tangled lights are a self-inflicted wound. Fix it this year:

  1. Test each string before you store it — a dead strand found in storage beats one found on the ladder.
  2. Wind each string onto a flat piece of cardboard or a cord reel so it can't tangle.
  3. Label each reel with its length and where it goes: "Front porch, 50ft," "Tree, warm white."
  4. Keep all the reels together in one Lights bin so you never hunt for them.

Label every bin — and make it searchable

A good label answers two things at a glance: a category up top ("Holiday — Outdoor") and a few key items below ("icicle lights, 2 wreaths, yard stakes"). But the real time-saver is being able to search. When each bin's contents live on your phone, you don't haul down six bins to find the nutcracker — you search its name, see it's in the Mantel bin, and grab only that one. A QR label on the bin turns "which box was that in?" into a two-second scan.

Do a quick inventory while it's all out

You'll never have a better moment to catalog the holiday stash than right now, with every bin open. Snap a photo of each bin's contents as you pack. Next November, you'll know exactly what you have (so you don't buy a third string of the same lights), what's getting tired and should be replaced in the after-season sales, and where every piece lives.

Frequently asked questions

What's the best way to store Christmas ornaments?

Use a bin with dividers or wrap each ornament in packing paper, keep breakables in their own clearly-labeled bin, and store that bin near the top of the stack so nothing heavy sits on it. Photograph the contents so you know what's inside without unwrapping everything.

How should you organize holiday decorations?

By area, not by type: a Tree bin, a Mantel bin, an Outdoor bin, a Table & kitchen bin. When each bin maps to a part of the house, decorating is just working through the bins.

How do you keep holiday lights from tangling?

Wind each string onto a flat piece of cardboard or a cord reel, test it before storing, and label it with its length and location. Keep all the reels in one bin.
